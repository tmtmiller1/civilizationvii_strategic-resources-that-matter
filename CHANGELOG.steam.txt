[b]Version 1.0.1 · 15 October 2026[/b]
The Catapult and the Trebuchet no longer need Niter. Neither one uses gunpowder, so in the Exploration Age Niter is now for the Bombard and the heavy warships. The license now says that Civilization VII and its art and data belong to Firaxis and 2K.

[b]Changed[/b]
• Exploration siege needs Niter from the third tier only: the Bombard and the Hwacha that replaces it. The Catapult, the Trebuchet and the two Hwacha that replace them are free of any requirement, with Gated Units on or off. The game's own +1 Combat Strength per Niter still applies to them, as in the base game, and so does the mod's extra strength past six.
• The LICENSE file ends with a notice that Sid Meier's Civilization VII and its assets belong to Firaxis Games and 2K, that the MIT license doesn't cover them, and that the game screenshots are there for illustration.

==== 1.0.0 | 2026-10-12 | src 4b60c70d4758 | gen 36432ca82911 ====
[b]Version 1.0.0 · 12 October 2026[/b]
Horses, Iron, Niter, Oil, Coal and Rubber matter again: going without one costs the units that need it, and can lock them.

[b]Added[/b]
• Unsupplied units. A unit that needs a strategic resource its owner has none of fights weaker and trains slower: -3 Combat Strength and a third less Production toward it by default. One of the resource lifts the penalty, and the game's own +1 for each applies as before. Melee strength always; ranged and bombard strength only on units that have them. Cavalry need Horses; infantry from the second tier need Iron; Exploration siege and heavy warships from the second tier need Niter; Modern artillery Niter, armour and battleships Oil, light warships Coal, aircraft Rubber. Unique units need what the unit they replace needs. Modern infantry need nothing. A resource that reaches a player over a trade route counts the same as a mined one, from the turn the route lands.
• Losing a resource hurts. The game notices a resource arriving but not one going away; the mod has it check every player again at the start of each turn, so a resource lost to a raid or a lost settlement brings the penalty back a turn later, for the AI players too.
• Past six, where the game's own bonus stops, units gain +1 more Combat Strength at 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 and 20 of a resource.
• Gated Units, off by default. With it on, a unit that needs a strategic resource cannot be trained or bought while its owner has none of it. It stays in the production list, greyed out, with a “Locked: no Iron” pill after its stats, and is locked again the moment the last one goes. Under Training and upgrade no unit can be upgraded into it either; under Training upgrades stay free. Units already in the field are never gated, and heal as usual. Independent powers and city-states are never gated. The AI is locked by the same rule and never sticks on a locked unit.
• Three game setup options: Unsupplied Units (Off, Light, Standard, Harsh; Standard by default), Strategic Resources Past Six (Off, On; On by default) and Gated Units (Off, Training, Training and upgrade; Off by default).
• The unit panel says when a unit is unsupplied: one more cell in its stats, the resource's icon over the penalty in red, with “Unsupplied: no Iron (-3 Combat Strength, slower to train)” as its tooltip; under Gated Units the tooltip ends with “New ones cannot be trained.”.
• The combat preview names the penalty as a line of its own, for both sides, for example “-3 Unsupplied: no Niter”, and the strength past six as one line beside the game's own, for example “+1 Niter: 8 or more”.
• The production list marks each unit that needs a resource with a pill like the game's own “Ageless”: “Unsupplied: no Niter” on a red tint while you have none, “Niter: 2” once you have some, “Locked: no Iron” under Gated Units. The item's hover tooltip carries the whole sentence under the unit's description, and on the purchase list it says what cannot be bought. The game's own text on the item is unchanged.
• A strategic resource's description, on the map and in the commerce screen, names the units it supplies for you in this age, for example “Supplies units: AT Gun, Assault Gun, Field Cannon and 3 more.”; under Gated Units, “Needed to train: ...”.
• A Civilopedia page under Game Concepts, Combat: the setup values, what counts as having a resource, losing one, past six, which units need what, and the gate. Each unit that needs a resource says so on its own page, with the penalty the game was set up with, and under Gated Units what it cannot be trained or bought without.
• A notification when you lose the last of a resource your units need, at the start of the next turn: “Supply lost: no Niter. Your Field Cannon and 3 other units fight at -3 and train slower until you get Niter back.”, with “New ones cannot be trained until then.” under Gated Units. Once per loss; it ends with the turn and never holds the turn up. Two losses in one turn share one notification.
• The first time you select one of your units while it is short of its resource, an advisor callout explains the rule and offers the Civilopedia page; under Gated Units a second callout, “Locked unit”, explains the lock. Each shows once, not in every game, and only with tutorials or advisor warnings on.
• Text in all twelve of the game's languages.
• Carries the Tower Settings Keeper, so every mod's settings survive a restart.

[b]Known limits[/b]
• Single-player. In multiplayer, losing a resource does not bring the penalty back until that player gains a resource.
• Under Gated Units, a unit already in the production queue when the resource is lost keeps its progress and is completed; the game checks the resource when a unit is queued or bought.
• A mod that sets the game's resource-minimum parameters itself keeps the engine's rule by its own numbers; this mod's lock texts then step back to their plain form.
